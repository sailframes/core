## The one-paragraph model

A sea breeze ("brise de mer") is a **closed local circulation** ("boucle de brise")
driven by land heating faster than the sea on a fair, lightly-clouded day. Warm land air
rises, cool sea air flows in at the surface (the breeze), a return current flows back out
aloft (a few hundred m up), and it subsides offshore. The advancing surface edge is the
**"front de brise."** It starts ~perpendicular to shore, then **veers right ~10°/hour**
(N. hemisphere, Coriolis), is **stronger at the coast**, and dies in the evening. At night
the gradient reverses and a weaker **land breeze ("brise de terre" / "brise nocturne")**
drains offshore, hugging the coast and funneling out of valleys. The whole behaviour is
reorganised by the background **synoptic wind** via the **theory of quadrants** (Ch. 3),
by **topography** (Ch. 5), and in hot regions by **thermal lows / "dépressions thermiques"**
(Ch. 6). Ch. 7 is forecasting (climatology, on-water observation, breeze polygons, and
high-res non-hydrostatic models like AROME).

## Key quantitative facts (fleet/tactics-relevant)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Sea-breeze right rotation (Coriolis, N. hem.) | **~10°/hour** |
| Establishment necessary conditions | air-over-land warmer than air-over-sea by **≥2–3 °C**; **synoptic < 18 kt** |
| Typical established sea-breeze speed | ~10–20 kt (up to 20–25 kt in favourable spots) |
| Loop first-branch / return-branch speeds (Fig 1) | surface **8–10 kt**, return aloft **15–20 kt** |
| Front de brise: forms offshore at | ~**3 M** (miles) off the coast |
| Front speed of advance | **2–3 kt**; nascent breeze behind it ≈ **2× front speed** (~6–8 kt) |
| Breeze vertical depth | **200–500 m** (our latitudes); 1000–1500 m in tropics |
| Breeze horizontal extent | **20–40 km** (our regions); 50–60 km Med; 80–150 km tropics |
| Loop max offshore extent (afternoon, ~17–18h) | **20–30 M** offshore |
| Transition zone (breeze↔synoptic) migration speed | ~**4–5 kt** offshore as breeze builds |
| Return-current wind proxy | the **850 hPa (~1500 m)** wind ≈ synoptic (off site effects) |
| Land breeze (nocturnal) speed | rarely > **10 kt**; confined ~**1–3 M** to coast; sets up 3–4 h after sunset, dies **1–2 h after sunrise** |
| Thermal low ("dépression thermique") pressure drop | ~**2–5 hPa** over overheated land; a Med phenomenon, ~absent in Channel/Atlantic |
| Synoptic ceiling for any breeze | **> ~18–20 kt** → sea breeze will not establish (turbulence destroys the loop) |

Establishment clock (pure breeze / quadrant 1): first hints late morning; established
early-to-mid afternoon; strongest & most coast-parallel mid-to-late afternoon; dies evening.

## Chapter 2 — Les brises thermiques : généralités (p.125–134)

- **1. La brise de mer** (p.125): fair-weather diurnal circulation, sea→land.
- **2. Les brises pures** (p.127): breeze driven purely by land–sea ΔT, weak synoptic.
  - **2.1 Conditions d'établissement**: primordial = weak synoptic; also unstable air mass over land, ΔT, coastline geometry.
  - **2.2 Comment s'établit la brise pure** (p.128, Fig 2 *Cycle d'établissement*): Phases 1→4 — offshore subsidence + land heating → loop forms → front forms ~3 M off → progresses inland. Panel speeds ~3 M / 4–6 kt / 2–4 kt / ~10 kt.
  - **2.3 Signes précurseurs** (p.129): **"nettoyage de l'horizon"** — offshore subsidence cleans/sharpens a hazy horizon; coastal cumulus form. (Fig 3: three photos hazy→sharp horizon + satellite coastal cumulus.)
  - **2.4 Établissement** (p.129): front reaches coast (~2–3 M off), moves inland; ahead = calm/erratic, behind = established breeze. (Fig 4 *Progression du front de brise*.)
  - **2.5 Évolution** (p.130): starts ⟂ coast, then **veers right ~10°/h**; relief complicates; right-shift near shore favours the inshore side. (Fig 5 *Rotation… et renforcement à la côte*.)
  - **2.6 Disparition** (p.131): dies as ΔT collapses or synoptic evolves; weakens over ~1 h, dies from offshore inward; parallel-to-coast component appears as it dies.
- **3. Brises thermiques nocturnes** (p.131):
  - **3.1 Brises nocturnes** (land breeze): needs clear-sky radiative cooling; weak; hugs coast (~2–5 M, Fig 6); very sensitive to modest coastal relief; drains out of valleys/river mouths ("drainage côtier"). **Fig 7**: colour relief map of N. Brittany (Île de Batz, Morlaix, Lannion, **Monts d'Arrée**) — cooled air drains seaward via coastal valleys. Interaction w/ synoptic: offshore-land wind < ~15 kt reinforces; sea wind < 15 kt opposes (breeze may fail except at valley mouths); sea wind > 15 kt overrides. Fig 8: nocturnal rotation + synoptic interaction cross-sections (23h/4h, "Synoptique venant de terre / faible du large / fort du large", transition bands 1–2 M / 1 M / 0.5 M).
  - **3.2 Vents catabatiques** (p.133, Fig 9): cold dense air draining downslope toward the coast; very valley-sensitive; reinforces the nocturnal breeze.
- **5. Résumé** (p.134): recap of pure breeze + land breeze as above.

## Chapter 3 — Influence du vent synoptique dans l'établissement de la brise (p.136–152)
### THE CORE STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

- **1. Mise en place de la brise et vent synoptique** (p.138)
  - **1.1 Le courant de retour en altitude**: a synoptic wind **from the land** *helps*
    (favours the aloft return current, "slips under" the synoptic); a synoptic wind
    **from the sea** *hinders* (blocks the return current). Fig 1: two "Phase 1"
    cross-sections, inclined isobars, red return-current arrows, "Dilatation de la masse
    d'air réchauffée."
  - **1.2 Divergence et convergence côtière** (p.139): coastal roughness contrast (land vs
    sea) induces **coastal divergence or convergence** depending on which side the coast is
    (looking upwind). **Coast on the right of the wind → divergence → subsidence → favours
    the breeze.** Coast on the left → convergence → ascendance. Fig 2 illustrates both.

- **2. La théorie des quadrants** (p.139): *"Premier temps"* — anything favouring the aloft
  return current favours establishment; that means **wind from the land AND coast-on-the-right
  divergence**. Quadrants are defined **relative to the coastline, NOT to compass points**
  (Fig 3 / Fig 15 *Positionnement des quadrants*, p.140/151). Looking out to sea:

  | Quadrant | Wind origin vs coast | Behaviour | Strategy |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Q1** | from land, coast on the **right** (return-current + divergence both help) | **Best.** Breeze establishes **early**, likely **strong**, builds from **coast → offshore**. | **Go to the coast.** |
  | **Q2** | from land, coast on the **left** (convergence hinders) | **"Combat douteux"** breeze vs synoptic. Confused, hard to predict; if breeze comes it's late and sets up a few miles **offshore first**; several failed attempts possible. | Play the **old wind**; only commit to the breeze once its arrival is certain (watch horizon clearing). |
  | **Q3** | from the sea, coast on the right | Impression the breeze **reinforces** the synoptic; wind does a **there-and-back** (backs left + softens as breeze sets in, then returns right). "Vraie brise" vs thermal-low effect hard to tell apart. | Nuanced; manage the transition zone. |
  | **Q4** | from the sea, coast on the left | **Worst.** Breeze usually **won't lift** despite promising heat; wind may die completely before returning as pure breeze. "Casse-tête." | Expect no help; consider anchoring/motoring. |

  - **2.1 Q1** (p.141, Fig 4 cross-sections 10/11/14–16h + Fig 5 rotation): morning = synoptic
    from land; ~9h coastal brassage; front forms ~11h; **14h breeze established 15–18 kt**,
    stronger at coast, sets ~20–30° off the general coast direction, max offshore extent
    20–30 M; veers right; ~17h30 diminishes from offshore, backs left toward synoptic.
  - **2.2 Q2** (p.143, Fig 6 *la brise se glisse sous le synoptique, sans se composer avec lui*
    — loop + "Transition calme"): the less-favourable case — land wind helps the return current
    BUT the coastal **convergence hinders** the offshore descending currents; the two roughly
    equal effects make the breeze **"se battre" (fight)** the synoptic ⟂ the coast. Breeze wins
    if: air unstable, synoptic not too strong (weaker synoptic → better odds), convergence not
    too strong, and ΔT (warmed coastal air vs cool offshore air) significant. If it establishes:
    morning light land wind = synoptic (check charts / 850 hPa ~1500 m); ~9 h solar coastal
    brassage (wind stronger in the coastal band, gusts further right); ~11 h breeze sets up
    (visible via offshore horizon clearing) while synoptic still reigns near the shore; front
    forms ~2–3 M off (air-from-sea meets warm coastal air), breeze behind it ~6–8 kt. Then Fig 7
    establishment cross-sections (p.144), Fig 8 *Rotation de la brise de quadrant 2* (p.145,
    Matin/Après-midi): morning "allers-retours brise-synoptique et calmes"; if breeze wins,
    return-to-synoptic is by the **right**; if synoptic wins, by the **left**.
  - **2.3 Q3** (p.145–146, Fig 9 establishment + Fig 10 rotation; Fig 11 *la brise se glisse
    sous le synoptique*): breeze slips under synoptic, offshore transition marked by calms;
    the wind does a day **round-trip** (backs left + softens at onset, returns right).
    **Cas 2 — effet de dépression thermique** (p.147, Fig 11 bis): if true breeze doesn't
    start, a thermal-low secondary effect gives a **~20° right veer + strengthening**, quasi-
    homogeneous over the 20–30 M band, gradual return to synoptic. Position depends on local
    topography; temporary left returns possible in the afternoon (Med examples).
  - **2.4 Q4** (p.149, Fig 12): if synoptic > 10 kt it softens a bit and veers slightly right
    in the afternoon; if < 10 kt it drops hard late morning and a (late, weak) pure breeze may
    eventually set up. Example: S winds S of the Loire → "campagne de mouillage."
  - **2.5 Secteurs intermédiaires** (p.150, Fig 13/14): wind **parallel** to coast (±~20°):
    - **Coast on the right** → afternoon heating **increases** the pressure gradient → wind
      **strengthens and backs left** ~20°, then returns to synoptic in the evening.
    - **Coast on the left** → heating **decreases** the gradient → wind **softens and veers**
      (~20°), possibly enough to fall into pure-breeze conditions.
- **3.2** Quadrant theory is far more relevant than "breeze+synoptic composition" for
  **synoptic < 12–14 kt**; between 15–20 kt the composition idea regains some value.
- **5. Résumé**: > 18–20 kt synoptic → little chance of breeze. Q1 breeze takes over &
  reshapes synoptic; Q2 fights it (confused); Q3 seems to reinforce it; Q4 refuses to lift.

## Chapter 4 — Phases transitoires: établissement, évolution, disparition (p.154–177)

- **1. La brise s'établira-t-elle?** (p.156) necessary: land-air > sea-air (2–3 °C enough) &
  synoptic < 18 kt. **Fig 1** *Les facteurs déclenchant la brise thermique* — Favorable↔
  Défavorable slider bars grouped as: initial local met (instability / low cloud / big ΔT
  favourable); topography (low coast, coastal marshes, **south-facing coast** favourable; high
  or north-facing unfavourable); large-scale (recent cold-front passage favourable; dry-air
  high unfavourable); orientation vs synoptic (**Q1 favourable, Q4 unfavourable**); visual
  cues (offshore clearing, inversion disappearing, wind twist, coastal cumulus favourable).
  - **Encadré HUMIDITÉ ET INSTABILITÉ VERTICALE** (p.157, Fig A): moist/saturated parcels are
    unstable (latent heat) → favour ascendance → favour the breeze (marshes, post-cold-front
    air). Worked parcel example: dry parcel to 500 m = 10 °C (colder than 11.8 °C standard →
    sinks); saturated parcel = 12.5 °C (warmer → keeps rising).
- **2. Comment s'établit la brise** (owes much to D. Wisdorff, "lois du moindre effort"):
  - **2.1 Synoptic nul** (p.158): breeze arrives from offshore subsidence zone; front + preceding
    calm form ~3 M off, move at 2–3 kt; breeze ≈ 2× front speed; direction set by coast-vs-sun
    orientation + local topo; front runs ~10 M inland.
  - **2.2 Influence de la topographie sur le front** (p.159): **"le front de brise est un grand
    paresseux"** — it advances by **least effort**: **blocks over headlands/pointes** (even low
    ones), **runs faster into bays**. *Classic trap: don't hug under the pointes on a fair
    morning — you sit becalmed while the fleet gets the new wind.* Figs 3–4: La Rochelle
    (blocks on Île de Ré, progresses via bays/Pointe des Minimes) and Quiberon.
- **3. Indices visuels d'apparition** (p.160):
  - **3.1** disappearance of the ~200 m marine **inversion** (grey haze) = positive sign.
  - **3.2 Nettoyage de l'horizon au large** = near-certain imminent-breeze sign (Fig 5).
  - **3.3 Torsion du vent** (p.161, Fig 6): breeze establishes aloft first, so **masthead vs
    boom wind direction differ** during onset → the two tacks trim differently and AWA differs
    (example: stbd Awa 28°/Bsp 6.8 vs port Awa 24°/Bsp 6.4). **Masthead wind further right ⇒
    imminent right shift** (arrives in 10–20 min). Corollary: don't calibrate the wand during
    onset.
  - **3.4 Loi du moindre effort** (p.162, Fig 7): the breeze establishes by the side giving the
    **smallest rotation** (Q1 nascent ~10:00 solar; Q2 ~11:00 with breeze/synoptic to-and-fro).
- **4. Cas d'exceptions**: (4.1) breeze arriving **with fog/brume** when air warm & water cold
  (ΔT ~6–8 °C; N Sea/Baltic — Dunkerque, Boulogne, Kiel); (4.2) breeze **forming at the coast**
  in very unstable Q1 air or over cold/shallow water — loop forms tiny near the beach then
  spreads (A→B→C, Fig 8); (4.3) **relatively strong synoptic** (Fig 9): with strong Q1 wind a
  warm front pushes offshore in the morning, then afternoon land-sea ΔP flips it to a
  cold-front-like breeze return, strong and late (San Francisco, Australia, Languedoc-Roussillon
  w/ upwelling).
- **5. Évaluation de la force de la brise** — **Wisdorff grid** (p.165–166):

  **Table 1 — criteria & weights**
  | # | Critère | Choix → Poids |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Air warmer than sea? | Oui = breeze possible / Non = no breeze |
  | 2 | Synoptic < 18 kt? | Oui = breeze possible / Non = no breeze |
  | 3 | Synoptic direction | Q1=2, Q2=1, Q3=0, Q4=−1 |
  | 4 | Air-mass stability | Unstable=2, Stable=−1 |
  | 5 | 800 m wind aids return current | Oui=1, Non=−1 |
  | 6 | Air-mass temperature | Cool=1, Mild=0, Hot=−1 |
  | 7 | Synoptic shifts toward breeze dir. (after onset) | Oui=1, Non=−1 |
  | 8 | Sunshine | Good/cumulus=1, Medium=0, Poor=−1 |
  | 9 | Afternoon high tide | Oui=0, Non=−1 |
  | 10 | Vertical shear aloft | Non=1, Oui=−1 |

  **Table 2 — total → max afternoon speed:** 8→25 kt, 7→20, 6→18, 5→16, 4→12, 3→9, 2→6, 1→unlikely.
  (Worked La Rochelle June example scores **3 → ~9 kt**.)
- **6. Quadrants et stratégie** (p.167): Q1 → go to the coast; Q2 → play old wind, only trust
  breeze once horizon clears; Q3 → manage the round-trip; Q4 → headache. Best synoptic proxy =
  **850 hPa (~1500 m) chart** (wetterzentrale, windy.com), else surface isobars, else the
  9–10 h wind.
- **7. Évolution** (p.168): general **right 10°/h (Coriolis)**; south/SW-facing coasts favoured
  (well-lit) vs north-facing. Fig 10: pure-breeze wind roses per coast orientation
  (E/S/W/N-facing), vectors labelled by clock hour, speed ref = 9 h solar wind. Fig 11:
  evolution per quadrant. Q1 backs left in establishment then veers right; Q2 veers right then
  backs left ~2 h (stronger when hot) then normal right; Q3 strengthens (maybe slight left)
  then normal right; Q4 unlikely, little change.
  - **7.3 Topography** channels the breeze on indented coasts → less free rotation.
- **8. Caractéristiques en fonctionnement**: (8.1) early oscillations regular ~10-min period,
  play them on the slow right rotation; by 15–16 h amplitude grows, less organised, longer
  period → treat shifts as persistent on short courses. (8.2) once formed the loop is
  **self-sustaining**, rejects synoptic aloft; the breeze↔synoptic **transition zone** (weak
  variable wind ≈ parallel to coast) migrates offshore at ~4–5 kt to 20–30 M by 17–18 h then
  back. Managing this matters most in **Q3** (Vendée SW example, Figs 12–13): morning transition
  passes offshore — don't sail it lengthwise, dodge to the coast for stable breeze; ~18 h it
  passes back — if synoptic usable (>10 kt) keep it and go offshore, else go coast to hold the
  breeze + set up for the night breeze.
- **9. Disparition** (p.172): (9.1) normal — wind drops from offshore, backs toward synoptic,
  air feels less cool/humid, offshore haze returns; be ready for hours of light wind before a
  possible night breeze; **stay near the coast**. (9.2) met change (approaching low, evening
  storms — altocumulus castellanus ~2 h warning). (9.3) return to synoptic follows **loi du
  moindre effort** (Fig 14): Q1 may briefly strengthen then die; Q2 → right if breeze won, left
  if synoptic won.
- **10. La brise nocturne** (p.173): recap — driven by chilled coastal air "flowing" seaward
  under warmer offshore air, amplified by slopes; rarely > 10 kt; veers right; drains through
  coastal notches.
- **11. Une régate autour de l'île de Ré** (p.173–177, Figs 15–17): worked case — returning from
  Phare des Baleines to La Rochelle as the breeze sets in. Fleet splits: **island side**
  (sheltered from foul current, tiny island breeze) vs **continent side** (cross the pertuis,
  fight current, then get the continental breeze). The front **blocks over Île de Ré** and
  progresses by least effort along the open pertuis → island boats sit becalmed while continent
  boats get the new breeze. Continent option loses ~0.5 M early (detour + current) but wins
  overall (~1 M behind then recovered at ~4 kt over ~6 M / 1h30). **Lesson: anticipation** —
  in light air low boat speed forbids mid-race strategy changes; pick the option right, early.

## Chapter 5 — Brises thermiques et topographie (p.178–194)

- **1. Reliefs côtiers et front** (p.179): front by least effort → faster in bays, blocks on
  promontories; once established, breeze force varies along the coast with how easily the front
  progressed; bad blocking can give **unexpected left returns**. **Fig 1 Alicante** (relief map):
  zone 1 breeze normal & right; zone 2 blocked by relief, weaker; zone 3 front struggles →
  advance/block/advance → **coherent left-returns you can play** (not noise).
- **2. Reliefs côtiers et force** (p.181): ideal coast = **low, low hills inland, marshes**
  (Languedoc-Roussillon, S England, S Brittany, Charentes). A **high coast (>200 m) dropping
  into the sea kills pure/Q1-Q2 breezes** (Côte d'Azur, Corsica — sunny but poor breezes). On a
  high coast cut by valleys, breeze sets up at valley mouths — sail a **"festooned" course**
  (Fig 2): hug the beach in low bays for the sea breeze, stand off the high capes for synoptic.
- **3. Vallées côtières** (p.182): breeze channels into the valley axis; Coriolis rotation
  suppressed inside the valley then snaps ~30° suddenly in the evening; night land breeze
  strongest at valley mouths (Baie de Morlaix, Plymouth). Fig 3 "Dévent/Diffluence" panels.
- **4. Baies de petite taille** (p.182): successive breezes — each side makes its own small
  divergent breeze first, then the general coastal breeze invades (stronger toward the mouth,
  ⟂ general coast), then veers right ("la brise suit le soleil"). Fig 4.
- **5. Baies de grande taille** (p.183): each shore plays its own quadrant game → **asymmetry**
  across the bay; central zone poorly ventilated. Fig 5.
- **6. Île proche de la côte** (p.184): competition island-breeze vs coastal breeze, calms
  between; note the **"point chaud"** — weak-wind hot spot to leeward of a heated island where
  the breeze detaches. Fig 6.
- **7. Île isolée** (p.185): needs enough thermal reserve. Small island → breeze erratic,
  stalls, restarts, returns to synoptic ~17–18 h. **Large island (radius > 30 M)** → each shore
  behaves like a straight coast. Figs 7–8: **Majorca** pure breeze — each coast makes its breeze,
  meeting in **convergence zones** (grey shaded, stationary cumulus; Palma airport marked).
- **8. Péninsules** (p.186): small → like a nearby island (Quiberon), quickly supplanted by the
  main coast (Fig 9). **Large (> ~50 M wide)** → each coast well established (Fig 10 **Cornwall**:
  narrow W of Falmouth → N & S fronts merge inland, cumulus/rain, breeze stalls & weak 10–12 kt;
  wide E of Falmouth → strong durable breezes). With synoptic apply quadrant rule per coast.
  - **8.3 Le Cotentin** (p.189) worked example, NE 10 kt: W coast (better vs NE, ~Q1) wins over
    E coast (Q4); when the W-coast front finally reaches the E coast the whole system can collapse.
- **9. Changements de direction importants** (p.190): pure breezes ≈ equal force each coast →
  **convergence line** (clouds/showers, reinforced breeze) if they meet, or **divergence line**
  (clear sky, weaker breeze) if they part. Fig 15: Calais/Cap Gris-Nez convergence vs
  St-Malo/Jersey divergence. **9.2 Rade de Brest** (Figs 16–18): local shore breezes first (calm
  in the middle), then the best-exposed Breton coast's breeze invades, strongly channelled
  (Élorn corridor / goulet), arriving late.

## Chapter 6 — Les dépressions thermiques (p.195–202)

- Regional-scale afternoon **thermal low** from strong heating; ΔP **~2–5 hPa**. Superposes on or
  replaces the classic breeze. **A Mediterranean phenomenon** (Var, Maures, Estérel, Sardinia,
  Baleares, Iberia), nearly absent Channel/Atlantic. Independent of vertical stability (works
  even in dry stable air, unlike the sea breeze). Can scale up to monsoons; Portuguese trades /
  Meltem are seasonal versions.
- **1.1 Conditions**: strong heating + classic breeze not properly established (dry air, or
  synoptic Q3/Q4 = from the sea). Wind force ∝ heating intensity.
- **1.2 Synoptique faible** (Fig 14): wind sets up toward the low around the hottest hour,
  **abruptly, no breeze-front**, then veers right (Coriolis) through the afternoon; stops
  ~18–19 h solar as ground cools. (**Buys-Ballot**: low pressure to the right looking upwind.)
- **1.3 Synoptique non négligeable**: land wind Q1/Q2 < 18 kt → classic breeze usually wins.
  Sea wind: **Q3** (Fig 2) — afternoon ΔP increase deforms isobars → wind **stronger (+5–10 kt)
  and further right at the coast** (first 3–5 M). **Q4** (p.199, Fig 3) — ΔP decrease → wind
  **softens ~5 kt at coast, ~10° more right offshore**, more unstable, unexplained left returns;
  can drop to zero → then a pure sea breeze may appear.
- **1.4 Difference vs classic breeze**: thermal low starts **abruptly over the whole zone** (no
  front); wind field has the characteristic right curvature; **force ~uniform over 3–5 M** (vs
  sea breeze stronger at the shore).
- **2. En Méditerranée** (p.200–202, Figs 4–7): ARPEGE 9 km @ 1-hPa isobars shows the diurnal
  cycle — morning NNE–NNW 8–10 kt; afternoon thermal lows over the southern reliefs (Var,
  Sardinia, Corsica/Ajaccio-Bonifacio) plus the classic Marseille breeze via the Rhône corridor;
  evening they relax with inertia. Convergence zone appears as a small closed low + cumulus →
  evening storms. Fig 4 = per-quadrant block-diagram summary.

## Chapter 7 — Prévision de la brise sur un plan d'eau (p.203–218)

- **1. Climatologie**: monthly means (Pilot Charts) useless for a diurnal oscillation; **12-h-or-
  finer stats** (Instructions nautiques, Wind Pilot) reveal a "good breeze coast" by comparing
  morning vs afternoon wind roses. **Table 1 — Belle-Isle (Le Talut) summer** (% by sector,
  N/NE/E/SE/S/SW/W/NW/Calmes): 07 h = 8/16/10/2/5/11/13/15/8; 13 h = 3/6/7/9/7/15/24/9/4;
  Δ = −5/−10/−3/+7/+2/+4/+11/−6/−4 → morning N-ish, afternoon W-ish + fewer calms ⇒ breeze coast.
- **2. Étude locale**: arrive early, "calibrate the plan d'eau" — try establishment/evolution
  hypotheses vs reality; rank the factors (relief vs coast orientation vs marsh stability vs
  tide). **2.3 Observations**: horizon clearing = near-certain onset; offshore cumulus line =
  loop in place (may be absent if dry air or Q3). **2.4 Polygones de brise** (Wisdorff): a polar
  diagram, one per synoptic direction; solar-hour vectors from a common point O give expected
  breeze dir+speed through the day; G = centroid = daily mean wind (Fig 3, example from *Le Vent
  dans les Pertuis*). 3-h step misses transient phases — combine with the least-effort laws.
  - **Encadré tide** (p.210): "wind comes with the tide" is **not** statistically supported;
    afternoon flood + breeze onset are usually just concomitant (fresh offshore water can add ΔT).
- **3. Modèles numériques**: global ~15 M mesh (GFS, ECMWF) → coarse regional breeze only;
  ~5 M (ARPEGE 9 km) → complex-coast detail; **< 2 M** → transient phases + fine coastal detail;
  **non-hydrostatic (AROME)** computes vertical velocities explicitly → best for the loop.
  Encadré p.211 explains hydrostatic vs non-hydrostatic. Figs 4–6: S Brittany case, GFS 15 M vs
  AROME 1.5 M interpolated to 3 M (Quiberon / Concarneau / Odet / Lorient points 1-2-3).
- **4. Limites**: stay cautious with doubtful establishment (marginal sun, small ΔT), transient
  phases (use least-effort laws), exact arrival time (watch the horizon, "not a train timetable"),
  post-establishment left returns (relief blocking or thermal-low deepening), afternoon synoptic
  change (evening storms). On meteograms (Windguru/Windy) pick a model < 5 M mesh.
- **5. Inshore Admiral's Cup 1991** (Isle of Wight, Nab Tower; p.214–217, Figs 7–9): worked race.
  Q2 NNE trailing air = "combat douteux" risk of no wind; as the ridge collapsed the wind backed
  to NW (→ Q1) and the sea breeze filled W/SW ~10 kt. **The breeze closes on the Isle of Wight
  headlands** ("se ferme sur les pointes") — boats chasing it at the pointes lose out; hold the
  old wind on the lifted tack; a valley mouth near the finish gave a stronger, further-right
  breeze. Take-aways: multi-scenario pre-analysis, continuous observation, hold a strategic line
  regardless of fleet position, a little luck.
- **7. Résumé**: many hard-to-rank parameters → "calibrate the plan d'eau"; high-res
  (esp. non-hydrostatic) models help a lot; best results = **models + observation + fundamentals**.

## Relevance to SailFrames (tactics / climatology / OCS)

- The **quadrant framework** (Ch. 3) is the natural strategic layer over the `/tactics`
  sea-breeze climatology and the RTMA/HRRR wind overlays — quadrant is defined relative to the
  **coastline orientation**, not compass, and predicts early-coast (Q1) vs offshore-first (Q2)
  vs round-trip (Q3) vs no-breeze (Q4) behaviour.
- **~10°/h right rotation**, **stronger-at-coast**, and the **offshore→onshore transition-zone
  migration (4–5 kt, out to 20–30 M by 17–18 h)** are concrete curves to compare against Boston
  Harbor fleet TWD/TWS tracks and the polar-plot TWD-bias work.
- **Onset cues** (horizon clearing, inversion loss, masthead-vs-boom wind twist ⇒ imminent
  right shift in 10–20 min) map to real-time start/pre-start reads and could inform OCS-window
  wind assumptions.
- **Wisdorff force grid** (Tables 1–2) = a cheap ready-made "will it fill / how hard" estimator
  for race-day briefings (Coach AI).
- **Non-hydrostatic high-res models (AROME < 2 M)** = the analogue of the HRRR 15-min sub-hourly
  reader already merged into the climatology briefing feed.
